# Ableton Push Chords

Chord shapes for the pad grid, shared by the chord files below. Push and Move both use this layout, so every shape plays the same on either.

## Files

- `ableton-push-theory-chords-basic.md`: triads and 7th chords
- `ableton-push-theory-chords-added.md`: 6ths, added 9ths, suspended chords and power chords
- `ableton-push-theory-chords-extended.md`: 9th, 11th and 13th chords
- `ableton-push-theory-chords-altered.md`: dominant 7th chords with an altered 5th or 9th
- `ableton-push-theory-chords-progressions.md`: chord progressions using these shapes

## Layout

- Chromatic note mode with the 4ths layout. One pad to the right is +1 semitone, and one row up is +5 (a 4th).
- Every shape works in every key. Put the shape's 1 on any pad and it plays that pad's chord.

## Reading the Diagrams

- Each diagram is a shape on the pad grid. The top line is the top row of pads, and the bottom line is the bottom row.
- Numbers are chord tones (1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and so on). `.` is a pad you don't play.
- Alterations come from the chord type, not the number. For example, in a Diminished Triad the 5 is a flat 5th.
- Each chord type starts with its chord symbol in C, followed by other common ways of writing it.
- An inversion heading says which chord tone is the lowest note: Root is the 1, First Inversion is the next chord tone up (the 3, or the 2 or 4 in suspended chords), Second Inversion is the 5, and Third Inversion is the 7 (or the 6 in 6th chords).
- Several diagrams under one heading are alternative shapes for the same inversion.
- Rootless sections leave out the 1, for when a bass or another instrument plays the root.
